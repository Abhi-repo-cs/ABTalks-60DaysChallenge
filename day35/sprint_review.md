Sprint Review & System Integration

Week 5 focused on transforming individual data science techniques into an integrated analytics workflow.

During this sprint, I worked with customer segmentation, product recommendations, anomaly detection, and dashboard development. The major objective was to understand how independent analytical components can be combined into a unified system that supports business decisions.

Customer segmentation helped identify groups of customers based on their purchasing behavior. RFM analysis provided useful features based on recency, frequency, and monetary value.

The recommendation module demonstrated how customer purchase history can be used to generate relevant product suggestions. Anomaly detection added another layer by identifying unusual transaction patterns that may require investigation.

The biggest challenge was integration. Each component has different inputs, outputs, and computational requirements. Instead of treating every model as an independent project, I designed a workflow in which preprocessing feeds multiple analytical modules and their outputs are brought together in a common dashboard.

Another important learning was understanding engineering tradeoffs. A more complex machine-learning solution is not always the best solution. For a production-style prototype, interpretability, speed, reliability, and ease of integration can be more valuable than unnecessary model complexity.

Streamlit helped turn the analytical pipeline into an interactive decision-support application. This made the results easier to communicate to non-technical users.

The most important lesson from Week 5 was:

A data science project becomes much more valuable when its analytical outputs are connected to real business decisions.

Overall, Week 5 improved my understanding of machine learning, customer analytics, dashboard development, software integration, and communicating data-driven insights.
