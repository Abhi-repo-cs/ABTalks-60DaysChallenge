# Week 6 Reflection

Week 6 helped me understand how separate analytics techniques can become much more useful when they are connected to a single business decision workflow.

I worked across KPI tracking, forecasting, retention analysis, experimentation, segmentation, and customer risk scoring. The main learning was that a strong analytical solution should not stop at producing charts or model predictions. It should explain what changed, why it matters, which customers or business areas require attention, and what action should happen next.

One of the most important lessons was the tradeoff between model complexity and explainability. A simple risk score can be easier for a business team to understand and act on, while a more advanced machine-learning model may provide stronger predictive performance if enough quality historical data is available.

I also learned that executive dashboards should be designed around decisions rather than around the number of metrics available. Too many charts can hide the most important signals. A good dashboard should make priorities visible quickly.

My next step is to improve the prototype using real historical data, validate predictive performance, add proper forecast evaluation, and connect recommendations to measurable retention experiments.

Overall, Week 6 shifted my perspective from building individual analytics projects to designing integrated decision-support systems.
